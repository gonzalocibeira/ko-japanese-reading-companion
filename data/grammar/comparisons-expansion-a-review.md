## Author checks

- Complete sentences include the needed verb and particle changes. The state/preparation family highlights both the predicate change and が/を; the purpose family highlights the whole changed goal phrase.
- The visible-state example uses **intransitive 開く + ている** and supplies **開い → あい**. The intentional-result/preparation examples use **transitive 開ける** and **開け → あけ**. Neither the content nor the question claims all ている is progressive, or all ている is a state.
- Purpose contrasts distinguish intended action, target ability, and prevention. They do not claim an absolute same-subject or verb-shape substitution rule for every ために/ように use. Root is broadening `n3-118` to cover purpose as well as cause so that its linked detail supports this lens.
- Decision language explicitly allows speaker participation in arrangements with ことになる and shared decisions with ことにする.
- はず and べき separate a supported expectation from a normative judgment; neither claims a guaranteed outcome. **九時 → くじ** is explicit.
- Recent completion includes overlap immediately after purchase, subjective timescales, and no invented clock threshold. The month-old shoe example is an original contextual illustration, not a source quotation.
- Optional questions have one unambiguous answer in their stated contexts. No scores, persistence, or progression are introduced.
